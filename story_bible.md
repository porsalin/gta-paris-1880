# GTA: Paris 1880 — Story Bible
## *L'Affaire du Tourbillon*: one story in five acts

**Logline.** In 1880, someone steals a master watchmaker's tourbillon regulator. His apprentice, Théo Marchal, follows the thief from the frozen Seine to the Opéra's hidden vault. Along the way he moves through the studios, salons, concert halls and galleries of a Paris where Impressionists, poets and composers are remaking art. The trail leads to a fictional financier's ring of thieves and forgers.

**Tone.** The game's heists are played as caper fiction rather than tragedy. Theft is mostly *re*-theft: forgeries are swapped out and stolen works are rescued, with the art returned to its makers and owners. Violence is aimed only at fictional criminals.

### Rules for historical figures (non-negotiable)
1. Real people appear only as **respectful speaking characters**: hosts, witnesses, mentors, passengers and patrons.
2. No real person is ever a murderer, victim, target, villain or criminal accomplice. Escorted real people are invulnerable. If the escort fails, the historical passenger *walks away shaken*; they never die.
3. Their dialogue is invented but in character. Where a line refers to an event, it is a real 1880 event: the 5th Impressionist exhibition, Manet and Monet at La Vie Moderne, Bernhardt leaving the Comédie-Française, *Les Soirées de Médan*, the July amnesty, the first 14 juillet, Rodin's *Gates of Hell* commission, and Offenbach's work on *Hoffmann*.
4. Real deaths that happened in 1880 (Flaubert in May, Offenbach in October) are shown only as public mourning. The plot never causes them.
5. All villains, crimes, artworks named as stolen or forged, and the conspiracy itself are **fictional**.

### The central mystery: the stolen tourbillon
Maître Vallet's tourbillon is not just a masterpiece. Baron Vaneau commissioned twelve chiming clocks for twelve halls of music: Pleyel, Érard, Viardot's salon, the Châtelet, the Opéra and others. Rung together in the right twelve-note order, the chimes open a sound-keyed vault beneath the Opéra Garnier. Only Vallet's escapement can drive the master clock that synchronises them. Inside the vault is everything the Baron's ring has stolen or forged over a decade: paintings, autograph scores, a famous violin, and the ledgers that buy him ministers and policemen.
- **Act I clue:** a magpie feather, a watch-key and lead white bought in forger's quantities.
- **Act II clue:** a cipher stave in invisible ink that turns out to be a chime. It is read by a young Conservatoire student and decoded with the help of Saint-Saëns in Act IV.
- **Twist:** La Pie, the Magpie who stole the tourbillon, is Odile, the Baron's ward. She took it to keep Vallet alive and becomes Théo's ally.
- **Red herring:** in Act IV the crew seizes the "tourbillon" from a ministry carriage. It is Crane's copy.
- **Resolution:** Théo sets the master clock twelve seconds slow. At midnight on Réveillon the city's bells ring the code before the Baron can, the vault opens to the crew, and everything goes home.

## Arc synopsis
- **Act I, Hiver: *L'Apprenti* (missions 1–20, January–March).**
  - The Seine thaws after the great freeze of 1879–80. Thieves break into Vallet's workshop; the master is hurt and the tourbillon is gone.
  - Théo recruits his painter friend Henri Vautrin. Through Maza, the fence of Les Halles, he learns the stolen-goods economy.
  - Lucie Arnoux, an exiled forger back from Geneva, reads the clues. Père Tanguy remembers a customer who bought lead white in bulk.
  - The crew discovers Silas Crane's forgery factory. Crooked Inspector Mourier frames Théo.
  - Baron Vaneau hires Théo to build him a clock, and the apprentice becomes an infiltrator.
- **Act II, Printemps: *Le Salon* (missions 21–40, April–May).**
  - Théo guards the Independents' exhibition for Degas and chases a copyist with Mary Cassatt. He hears Maupassant's verdict on the "Baron".
  - At the Salon, Théo spirits away canvases that Vaneau wants to "lose". He shadows Manet's show at La Vie Moderne.
  - The invisible-ink stave goes from Morisot's salon to the Conservatoire, where the young Debussy reads it: it is a chime.
  - Pissarro teaches Théo how to see a forgery. Crane's notebook names the twelve bells.
- **Act III, Été: *La Fête* (missions 41–60, June–August).**
  - The amnesty brings the exiles home, Monet's ice paintings arrive at La Vie Moderne, and Paris holds its first 14 juillet.
  - Théo sabotages the bell receivers at Pleyel and at Viardot's Thursday. He corners La Pie on the rooftops: it is Odile.
  - Rodin, Nadar and Garnier each give the crew a piece of the puzzle. Caillebotte lends a boat-shed as the **safehouse gallery**.
  - Théo goes down to the lake under the Opéra, and steals a manuscript forgery from the Bibliothèque nationale.
- **Act IV, Automne: *Les Contes* (missions 61–80, September–November).**
  - Offenbach needs a watchmaker for the Olympia automaton in *Hoffmann*. After his death, Paris mourns at the Madeleine.
  - Saint-Saëns realises the chime is a combination, and Debussy hears its reverse.
  - The Drouot job takes the sale ledgers. Hijacking the ministry carriage yields only Crane's copy.
  - Vaneau burns the workshop and orders Théo to kill Odile; Théo fakes her death.
  - Chabrier's waltz and Victor Hugo's blessing to "publish" set up the endgame.
- **Act V, Réveillon: *Minuit* (missions 81–100, December).**
  - The crew forges invitations, steals costumes and swaps the master escapement. They clear the watchmen at the twelve halls and make Mourier late.
  - At the gala the bells ring, the vault opens and the treasures escape by omnibus to the safehouse.
  - Crane is caught at the Gare de l'Est and Mourier falls at the Préfecture. The Baron is beaten in the Batignolles yards, and Odile is freed.
  - The tourbillon goes home. Degas sees the rescued works, Odile leaves on Bernhardt's tour, and the year ends with the tourbillon striking twelve on rue Mouffetard.

## Systems that carry the story
- **Chapter title cards:** a full-screen card opens each act with its act number, season, title and premise.
- **One linear arc:** each story mission unlocks only after the previous one. The four contacts' magneto phones (Vautrin, Maza, Lucie, and Vaneau when Théo is undercover) ring for the next mission only; the others say who is waiting for you.
- **Heist mechanics (◆ in the mission list):**
  - **[STEALTH]**: an alarm or a raised wanted level fails the job.
  - **[FORGERY SWAP]**: a single stealth swap of the forgery for a marked copy.
  - **[FRAGILE CARGO]**: the job fails if the vehicle takes more than 30 damage.
  - **Alarm**: survive the bells.
  - **Fencing**: a cash payout at Maza's.
- **Safehouse gallery** (pause menu → GALLERY): it fills with the rescued paintings, scores, the violin, the evidence and finally the tourbillon.
- **Codex** (pause menu → CODEX): the Dramatis Personae list. Historical figures are marked *[historical]*.
- **Side jobs (kiosque):** cab races, a piano delivery, sheet-music runs, copyists at the Louvre, an omnibus line and more.

## Dramatis Personae
### Fictional
- **Théo Marchal**: Protagonist. A watchmaker's apprentice, 19, from the rue Mouffetard. Quick hands, a good ear, and a debt he did not choose.
- **Maître Augustin Vallet**: Théo's master, a horologist of genius. His masterpiece, a tourbillon regulator, is stolen in the first scene.
- **Henri Vautrin**: A penniless painter at the rue Ravignan piano works. Théo's oldest friend; knows every studio and every model in Montmartre.
- **Maza**: Fence and fixer of Les Halles, holding court at Au Chien qui Fume. Buys anything, sells everything, keeps her word.
- **Lucie Arnoux**: Printer, engraver and reformed forger, back from exile in Geneva. Can copy any signature, refuses to copy a conscience.
- **Baron Léopold Vaneau**: VILLAIN. Financier, collector, patron of the Opéra. Behind the velvet: a ring of thieves, forgers and bribed officials.
- **Silas Crane**: VILLAIN. Vaneau's English forger and cracksman. Paints like an angel, lies like a ledger.
- **Inspecteur Gaspard Mourier**: VILLAIN. A Préfecture detective on Vaneau's payroll who hunts Théo through all five acts.
- **La Pie (Odile Vaneau)**: The Magpie: a masked cat-burglar working for the Baron. His ward, and not as loyal as he believes.
### Historical (speaking cameos, always respectful)
- **Edgar Degas**: Painter. Organiser of the Independents' exhibition on rue des Pyramides. Dry wit, sharp eye, little patience for fools.
- **Mary Cassatt**: Painter, exhibiting with the Independents. Practical, generous, and the first to believe Théo.
- **Berthe Morisot**: Painter, exhibiting with the Independents. Her salon is a refuge of good sense.
- **Gustave Caillebotte**: Painter, collector and quiet benefactor of his friends' careers.
- **Camille Pissarro**: Painter; a patient mentor to younger artists.
- **Claude Monet**: Painter, mostly at Vétheuil this year; his June show at La Vie Moderne brings him to Paris.
- **Pierre-Auguste Renoir**: Painter; this year he sends to the official Salon.
- **Édouard Manet**: Painter; his spring show at La Vie Moderne is the talk of the boulevard.
- **Paul Durand-Ruel**: Art dealer, rue Le Peletier; staking his fortune on the Impressionists.
- **Julien 'Père' Tanguy**: Colour merchant of rue Clauzel; extends credit to painters in paint, and in kindness.
- **Émile Zola**: Novelist; Nana is serialised this year. An observer who notices everything.
- **Guy de Maupassant**: Writer; 'Boule de Suif' makes his name this spring.
- **Victor Hugo**: Poet, 78, the grand old man of the Republic; receives Théo with courtesy.
- **Stéphane Mallarmé**: Poet; his Tuesday evenings on rue de Rome.
- **Sarah Bernhardt**: Actress; leaves the Comédie-Française this spring and plans an American tour.
- **Jacques Offenbach**: Composer; working on Les Contes d'Hoffmann.
- **Pauline Viardot**: Singer and composer; hosts musical Thursdays.
- **Camille Saint-Saëns**: Composer, pianist and organist.
- **Gabriel Fauré**: Composer; choirmaster at La Madeleine.
- **Jules Massenet**: Composer; professor at the Conservatoire.
- **Claude Debussy**: A gifted 18-year-old student at the Conservatoire.
- **Emmanuel Chabrier**: Composer, newly resigned from the Ministry to write music full time.
- **Auguste Rodin**: Sculptor; commissioned this summer for a great portal, the Gates of Hell.
- **Nadar**: Photographer and balloonist, boulevard des Capucines.
- **Charles Garnier**: Architect of the Opéra, who knows its every cellar.

101. **Courses de Fiacres** (Cab Races): Race a fiacre across the Right Bank.
102. **Le Receleur de Nuit** (Night Fence): Fence the loot from Les Halles.
103. **L'Accordeur** (The Piano Tuner): Deliver a grand piano without a scratch.
104. **Les Copistes** (The Copyists): Catch art students copying in the Louvre and swap their copies for blanks before Crane buys them.
105. **L'Omnibus** (The Omnibus): Run the Madeleine–Bastille omnibus line.
106. **Les Apaches de Belleville** (Belleville Apaches): Clear Belleville of Velvet extortionists.
107. **Le Tramway** (The Tram): Ride the tram and keep the pickpockets off it.
108. **Les Moulins** (The Windmills): A rooftop race across the Montmartre windmills.
109. **L'Alarme** (The Alarm): Test the Banque de France's new alarm, for a fee.
110. **Les Partitions** (Sheet Music): Deliver scores to the publishers before the concerts.
111. **Le Cirque** (The Circus): Escort a horse to the Cirque Fernando.
112. **La Tour Saint-Jacques** (Tour Saint-Jacques): Hold the tower square.
